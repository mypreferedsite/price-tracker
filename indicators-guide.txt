=====================================================================
 GUIDE TO THE 6 CHARTS IN YOUR PRICE TRACKER
=====================================================================

IMPORTANT FIRST
- This is an explanation of how to read charts. It is NOT financial
  advice, and I am not a financial advisor.
- No indicator predicts prices. Each one describes what the price HAS
  been doing, and traders use that as a hint about what MAY happen.
- Indicators are often wrong. Never rely on one alone, and never risk
  money you cannot afford to lose.

All charts in your app use ONE PRICE PER HOUR, in USD, taken from
the prices your tracker saved. "Hours" in the settings means hourly
points (10 hours = the last 10 hourly prices).


---------------------------------------------------------------------
 1. TWO MOVING AVERAGES  (a "fast" line and a "slow" line)
---------------------------------------------------------------------
WHAT IT IS
A moving average (MA) is the average price over the last N hours. It
smooths the small ups and downs so you can see the direction.
  - FAST average (short, e.g. 20 hours): follows the price closely.
  - SLOW average (long, e.g. 50 hours): shows the bigger trend.
  - SMA = simple average. EMA = gives more weight to recent prices, so
    it reacts faster.

HOW TO READ IT
  - Price above both lines, lines rising      -> uptrend.
  - Price below both lines, lines falling     -> downtrend.
  - Fast line crosses ABOVE the slow line     -> green triangle on the
    chart. Traders call it a possible BUY signal ("the trend may be
    turning up").
  - Fast line crosses BELOW the slow line     -> red triangle. A
    possible SELL signal ("the trend may be turning down").
  - Lines tangled together and crossing often -> the price is moving
    sideways. The signals are mostly noise then.

LIMITS
  - It LAGS: the signal arrives after the move has already started.
  - In a sideways market it gives many false signals.

SETTINGS IN THE APP
  Average type (Simple / Exponential), Fast hours, Slow hours.
  The fast value should be smaller than the slow one.


---------------------------------------------------------------------
 2. RSI  (Relative Strength Index)
---------------------------------------------------------------------
WHAT IT IS
A number between 0 and 100 that measures how fast and how strongly
the price has risen or fallen recently.

HOW TO READ IT
  - Above 70  -> the price rose fast. It may be "stretched" (overbought)
                 and could pull back. It is NOT an automatic sell.
  - Below 30  -> the price fell fast. It may be "oversold" and could
                 bounce. It is NOT an automatic buy.
  - Around 50 -> balanced, no strong push either way.
  - In a strong uptrend RSI can stay above 70 for a long time. In a
    strong downtrend it can stay below 30. So use it with the trend.

LIMITS
  - It works best when the price moves up and down in a range. In a
    strong trend, its warnings come too early.

SETTINGS IN THE APP
  RSI hours (7 = faster and jumpier, 14 = standard, 21 = smoother).


---------------------------------------------------------------------
 3. MACD  (Moving Average Convergence Divergence)
---------------------------------------------------------------------
WHAT IT IS
It compares two exponential averages (12 hours and 26 hours). The
difference is the MACD line. A third, smoother line (9 hours) is the
SIGNAL line. The bars show the gap between MACD and the signal line.
(These 12 / 26 / 9 values are the standard ones and are fixed.)

HOW TO READ IT
  - MACD line crosses ABOVE the signal line -> momentum turning up
    (green bars appear above the zero line).
  - MACD line crosses BELOW the signal line -> momentum turning down
    (red bars appear below the zero line).
  - Bars getting SHORTER means the push is weakening, even if the price
    is still moving the same way.
  - MACD above zero means the short-term trend is above the long-term
    trend. Below zero means the opposite.

LIMITS
  - Like the moving averages, it lags. It gives many false crosses when
    the price moves sideways.

SETTINGS IN THE APP
  None (fixed at 12 / 26 / 9).


---------------------------------------------------------------------
 4. BOLLINGER BANDS
---------------------------------------------------------------------
WHAT IT IS
A middle line (a simple moving average) with an upper and a lower band
around it. The bands are placed a number of "standard deviations" away,
which simply means: the more the price swings, the wider the bands.

HOW TO READ IT
  - Price near the UPPER band   -> stretched up. Could cool off.
  - Price near the LOWER band   -> stretched down. Could bounce.
  - Price riding along the upper band in a strong trend is normal.
    Touching a band is not a signal by itself.
  - Bands NARROW (squeeze)      -> the market is quiet. A bigger move
    often follows, but the bands do not tell you which direction.
  - Bands WIDE                  -> the market is volatile. Use smaller
    positions or wider stop-losses.

LIMITS
  - A touch of the band is not a reason to buy or sell. Price can walk
    along a band for a long time.

SETTINGS IN THE APP
  Bands hours (10 / 20 / 50) and Width (1.5 / 2 / 2.5). Wider = fewer
  touches. The standard setting is 20 hours and width 2.


---------------------------------------------------------------------
 5. LINEAR REGRESSION (trend line)
---------------------------------------------------------------------
WHAT IT IS
A straight line drawn through the prices of the selected period (Day,
Week, Month, Year or All) so that, on average, it is as close to the
prices as possible. Around it is a channel: a band 1, 1.5 or 2 "sigmas"
wide. A sigma (standard deviation) is the typical distance of the price
from the line, so most prices sit inside a 2-sigma channel.

HOW TO READ IT
  - Line sloping up   -> over this period the price trended up.
    Line sloping down -> it trended down. The page shows the slope in
    USD per day and in percent per day.
  - R-squared (R2)    -> how straight the trend is. 1 means the prices
    follow a perfectly straight line. Near 0 means there is no straight
    trend and the line says very little. Below about 0.3 the page warns
    you.
  - Price near or above the upper channel -> well above its trend.
    Price near or below the lower channel -> well below its trend.
    Some traders see this as "stretched" and expect a return to the
    line. Others see a breakout of a new trend. The chart cannot tell
    which one will happen.
  - The page also shows where the line would be in 24 hours if the trend
    simply continued. That is arithmetic, NOT a forecast.

LIMITS
  - The line changes when you change the period. A Week line and a
    Month line can point in different directions, and both are correct
    for their own period. Look at several periods.
  - Prices are not straight lines. A good-looking line over a short
    period can be just noise.
  - It describes the past. Trends end, often without warning.

SETTINGS IN THE APP
  Trend channel (1, 1.5 or 2 sigma). The period is controlled by the
  Day / Week / Month / Year / All buttons.


---------------------------------------------------------------------
 6. BACKTEST
---------------------------------------------------------------------
WHAT IT IS
It replays a simple rule on your saved price history and shows what
WOULD have happened. The chart starts at 100 at the beginning of the
selected period. The orange line is the rule. The grey line is simply
holding the asset the whole time.

THE RULES YOU CAN TEST
  - Fast average above slow average : in the asset while the fast line
                                      is above the slow line, in cash
                                      otherwise.
  - RSI                             : buy when RSI < 30, sell when > 70.
  - MACD                            : in the asset while MACD is above
                                      its signal line.
  - Bollinger Bands                 : buy below the lower band, sell when
                                      the price rises above the middle.

WHAT THE NUMBERS MEAN
  - Closed trades : how many buy-then-sell rounds were completed.
  - Winning       : the share of closed trades that made money.
  - Rule result   : total gain or loss of the rule in the period.
  - Just holding  : gain or loss if you had simply kept the asset.
  - Worst drop    : the biggest fall from a high point of the orange
                    line. It tells you how much pain you would have
                    had to sit through.
  - Cost per trade: a fee or spread (in %) taken at each buy and sell.
                    Keep it above 0, otherwise results look too good.

HOW TO JUDGE IT
  - A rule is only interesting if it beats "just holding" AFTER costs,
    with a worst drop you could live with.
  - Fewer than about 10 closed trades means the result is luck, not
    proof. The app shows a warning. Use Month / Year / All and load
    past prices to get more history.
  - The backtest looks at the past only. A rule that worked before can
    fail tomorrow.
  - Do NOT keep changing the settings until the result looks good. That
    is called overfitting: it fits the past perfectly and fails on new
    data. Choose sensible settings first and test them once.
  - It assumes you could buy and sell at the saved hourly price, with
    no delays. Real trading is a little worse.


---------------------------------------------------------------------
 HOW TO USE THEM TOGETHER (a simple routine)
---------------------------------------------------------------------
1. TREND: use the two moving averages. Is the price above or below the
   slow average? Trade with that direction rather than against it.
2. TIMING: look at RSI and MACD. Do they agree with the trend? For
   example, in an uptrend, an RSI dip toward 30-40 or a MACD turning up
   again can mark a better entry than buying after a big jump.
3. CONTEXT: look at the linear regression. Is the price far above or
   below its trend line, and is the trend straight enough (R2) to
   matter? A price far outside the channel can mean "stretched" or
   "new trend", so confirm it with the other charts.
4. RISK: look at the Bollinger Bands. Wide bands = big swings, so use a
   smaller position and a wider stop-loss.
5. TEST: before trusting any rule, check the Backtest over a long
   period (Month / Year / All).
6. ALERT: set a price alert or a "fast crosses slow" alert so the page
   tells you when something changes (only while the page is open).

Do not stack many indicators that say the same thing. MACD and the
moving averages, for example, are both built from averages, so they
often agree. Using one for trend, one for momentum (RSI) and one for
volatility (Bollinger Bands) gives more different information.


---------------------------------------------------------------------
 THE SYNTHESIS PANEL (top of asset.html)
---------------------------------------------------------------------
It combines the 5 indicators the way a trader reads them: first the
DIRECTION, then whether the price is cheap or expensive right now.

  DIRECTION (trend) : two moving averages, MACD, linear regression.
  STRETCH           : RSI, Bollinger Bands, and the distance of the
                      price from the regression line.

Each indicator gives a vote from -1 (bearish) to +1 (bullish). The
direction score and the stretch score are then read together:

  Uptrend + not stretched   -> BUY bias (pullbacks are the usual entry)
  Uptrend + stretched       -> HOLD, do not chase the price
  Downtrend + not oversold  -> SELL / stay out
  Downtrend + cheap side    -> WAIT: a bounce is possible, but trading
                               against the trend is risky
  No clear trend            -> NEUTRAL (watch the edges of the range)

The score from -100 to +100 shows how strong the picture is.
"Confidence" depends on how many of the 5 agree and on whether the
data is good enough (enough history, R2 not too low).

The panel also shows the volatility (Bollinger width), the levels where
the view would weaken, a backtest reality check, and a reminder about
news events. It is a reading aid, not a prediction and not advice.
When the indicators disagree, the honest answer is "no edge": do not
force a trade.


---------------------------------------------------------------------
 THE THREE ANALYSIS PAGES
---------------------------------------------------------------------
currencies.html (CHF, EUR, JPY, GBP), metals.html (gold, silver,
platinum) and crypto.html (Bitcoin, Ethereum) all have the same 6 charts,
the synthesis panel, alerts and table. Each is tuned to its market:

  CURRENCIES  Closed on weekends (those hours are skipped). Range-bound
              markets, so the "cheap or expensive" side counts a bit more.
              The page adds a DOLLAR CHECK: if all four currencies move the
              same way, it is the dollar moving, not one country.
  METALS      Closed on weekends. The page adds the gold/silver ratio and
              the platinum/gold ratio. Silver and platinum swing more than
              gold because industry also drives them.
  CRYPTO      Open 24/7, no weekend gaps. Trends run further, so the trend
              indicators count more, RSI uses 25 / 75 as its limits, and
              the default averages are faster (12 / 48, exponential). The
              page adds the ETH/BTC ratio. Swings are far larger: use
              smaller positions.

These tunings are reasoned choices, not proven ones. Judge them with the
Backtest chart and your own record of signals.


---------------------------------------------------------------------
 THE RATIO PAGE (ratio.html)
---------------------------------------------------------------------
Measures one asset in another: gold in euros, gold in Bitcoin, ETH in
BTC, gold/silver... Pick the two assets, or tap a preset.

WHY IT IS USEFUL
  - It removes the dollar. If gold rises in dollars but not in euros,
    the dollar was the main reason.
  - It shows which of two assets is stronger (rising ratio = the first
    one gains on the second), which helps to choose between them.
  - Gold against Bitcoin works as a mood gauge: safe haven versus risk.
  - It shows your purchasing power in the money you actually use.

LIMITS
  - It does not tell you whether either asset will rise in dollars.
    Both can fall while the ratio rises.
  - Links between assets change and can break for months.
  - It is just another price chart: the same indicators apply, and the
    same limits too.

The page shows the ratio with two averages and Bollinger Bands, the RSI
of the ratio, an advice panel (which asset leads, how stretched the
ratio is, what moved, where the view weakens) and an hourly table.


---------------------------------------------------------------------
 EXTRA TOOLS ON THE ANALYSIS PAGES
---------------------------------------------------------------------
DAILY VIEW
  Besides the hourly verdict, the advice panel looks at the DAILY trend
  (price against its 50-day average, and the 20-day against the 50-day
  average). When the hourly view goes against the daily trend, the
  confidence drops: such a move is usually only short-term. It needs
  about two months of saved history.

DOLLAR CHECK
  On the metals and crypto pages, a basket of euro, yen, pound and franc
  (weighted like the dollar index) shows whether the dollar was
  stronger or weaker over the chosen period. A stronger dollar tends to
  weigh on gold and crypto, but other news often breaks this link.

RISK CALCULATOR
  Enter your capital, the percentage you accept to lose, your entry price
  and your exit (stop) price. It gives the size of the position, the
  maximum loss if the exit is hit, and prices for gains of 1x and 2x the
  risk. It warns when the position is larger than your capital (that needs
  borrowing, which multiplies losses). It is arithmetic only.

OLD-DATA WARNING
  A red or orange banner appears when the saved prices are old, for
  example because the collector stopped. Weekends are taken into account
  for metals and currencies.

TRACK RECORD (track.html)
  Rebuilds the verdict for every past hour from the saved prices, using
  only what was known at that hour, and shows what the price did 24
  hours, 72 hours and a week later. A BUY verdict is only useful if the
  price did better afterwards than after an average hour, and a SELL
  verdict if it did worse. Hours overlap and the history is short, so it
  is a first impression, not proof.

DATA HEALTH (health.html)
  Shows how old the last price is, which source each price came from, and
  gaps in the history for every asset, with a plain-English OK or Check.


---------------------------------------------------------------------
 THINGS NO INDICATOR CAN SEE
---------------------------------------------------------------------
- Central bank interest-rate decisions (US Fed, ECB, Bank of England,
  Bank of Japan, Swiss National Bank).
- Inflation and jobs reports, especially from the US.
- The overall strength of the US dollar.
- Wars, crises and sudden news. Gold and the Swiss franc often rise
  when investors are afraid.
Big announcements can move prices much more than any indicator
suggests. Many traders reduce or close positions before them.


---------------------------------------------------------------------
 LIMITS OF YOUR TRACKER'S DATA
---------------------------------------------------------------------
- Prices come from free sources and can be delayed.
- Currency prices on the live page update slowly, so do not use them
  for very fast trading.
- Hourly points mean the indicators react to slow moves, not to
  minute-by-minute ones.
- The history is only as long as you have saved or loaded. Short
  history makes every indicator less reliable.

Good habits matter more than any signal: decide in advance how much
you can afford to lose, where you will exit if you are wrong, and keep
each trade small compared to your total money.
